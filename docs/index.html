Speaker grille

2026-08 · remix table · 1 pin round · render: Gemini, over the locked stencil
7 · the same geometry, rendered — walnut, bone, brass

A real part, engineered while it was designed: a grille for real speakers that has to transmit sound perfectly, stay safe for toddler fingers, and come off for cleaning and service. It started as one rough 48 mm circle and a pin note — “6 mm holes center, 3 mm toward the edges” — and the agent computed the 90-hole field with 3 mm webs, verified in the file. One pin round — and a lot of debate about the physics — because every hole size is an acoustics and safety decision, not decoration. The flange, standoffs, and screw bosses came in the same pin loop, and because the result is plain SVG geometry it accepts any manufacturing technique — and invites community mods. Claude built the print mesh straight from that geometry: two minutes to a printable part, no Fusion modeling at all. Gemini rendered the locked stencil in walnut, bone, and brass — same split as everywhere on this page: vector studio makes the geometry, Gemini makes the picture. Ninety holes of varying sizes is time-consuming for a human to design and manage alone — here it was a sentence and a handful of pins.

Landscape — storyboarding from a plan view

2026-08 · plan view → human perspective → render: Gemini

Built for storyboarding, where the hard problem is holding one viewpoint across scenes. So the scene starts as a plan view — trees as dots, the path as a polygon — and the agent interprets that map from a human perspective into the line work; fills follow, and Gemini renders the frame. Because the camera is derived from the plan, the next scene reuses the same map and stays consistent. Quick, and extremely powerful — and since every tree is its own shape, the same file also splits into a five-layer parallax.

Snowflake — a first-timer's first drawing

2026-08 · one arm + a center point · render: Gemini

My wife’s first drawing in the tool — and her first in any vector, CAD, or photo-editing software. The instructions were one sentence: draw one side of a snowflake and mark the center. The agent mirrored and arrayed her arm into full six-fold symmetry; the filled silhouette is exactly that geometry. To be plain about the split: the ice render is Gemini’s — vector studio makes geometry, not renders.

The gadz.tech logo — a known design

2026-08 · 3 hours the old way, 2–3 minutes this way

This shape is why the tool exists — it’s the gadz.tech logo, rebuilt as a test against a design that already existed. The original took three hours of describing the shape to a chat session, then Fusion work out of frustration, with the wrong file back three times. In vector studio it took two or three minutes: one wedge on the mm grid, the agent placed the other eleven — and once it lived as clean geometry, animating it was the fun part. The fix wasn’t a better description; it was pointing at geometry instead of describing it.
